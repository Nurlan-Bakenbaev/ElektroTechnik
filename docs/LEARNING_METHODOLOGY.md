# Teaching electrical engineering through action

The reference lesson follows Problem → Observe → Predict → Experiment → Explain → Apply → Check → Reflect. It is designed for beginners who know the words voltage, current and resistance but have not yet connected them mathematically.

1. Present a 12 V circuit and ask what a larger resistor changes.
2. Activate prior knowledge: distinguish charge/time from energy/charge; offer a short refresher.
3. Open and close a switch and interpret both instruments. The voltage source retains its voltage when the circuit is open.
4. Ask for a prediction before measuring; an incorrect prediction is retained and can still lead to the experiment.
5. Require three different closed-circuit measurements at 12 V. Recording a duplicate does not advance the activity.
6. Explain the observed inverse relation in plain German. Clarify that charge is not consumed and electrons are already present in a metal conductor.
7. Show the sourced formula image and let learners choose U, I or R to inspect meanings and units.
8. Guide a 24 V / 8 Ω calculation through unknown quantity, calculation method and numerical result. Later questions remain hidden until the prior decision is correct.
9. Use three independent numerical tasks with changed unknowns and units.
10. Check meter connections, symbol/unit matching, multiple model assumptions, measurement interpretation and error detection.
11. Record a learner-written explanation. The software saves this reflection but does not pretend to grade its meaning.
12. Transfer to resistor selection and power dissipation, then the existing five-question topic quiz.

The bank contains 13 distinct checks. Hints are recorded. Wrong answers receive a relevant principle or unit diagnostic, and can be retried; a worked solution is optional after two submitted attempts. Only the original final quiz updates course XP, so repeated practice does not inflate the profile.

The Ohm model assumes a constant temperature and constant resistance. It must not be generalized to an incandescent filament, diode or reactive AC circuit without changing the model. Sources are linked directly from the model notes.
